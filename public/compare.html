Card Rails vs Stablecoin

Card Rails Stablecoin (USDC)
ModelPull: merchant requests the debitPush: customer initiates the transfer
FlowAuth then capture (2 steps)Single transaction (1 step)
SettlementT+2 (sometimes T+1)~15 seconds
AvailabilityTransactions 24/7, settlement on banking business days24/7/365
FinalityProvisional for 120 days (chargeback window)Irreversible once confirmed
ChargebacksYes: cardholder can disputeNo dispute mechanism
RefundsSame rail in reverseNew transfer initiated by the merchant
CustodyBank (issuer/acquirer)Depends on model: self-custody, custodial, or shared (MPC). This demo uses dev-controlled shared custody via Circle.
Merchant feesMDR 1.5-3% (interchange + scheme fees + acquirer margin)None at protocol level (gas is paid by the sender). Infra provider may charge separately.
Sender feesNone for the cardholderGas (~$0.01-0.50 depending on network)
IntermediariesIssuer, network (Visa/MC), acquirer, processorBlockchain validators only
Cross-borderFX surcharge 1-3%Same gas cost regardless of geography. FX cost shifts to on-ramp/off-ramp.
ReconciliationSettlement files, batches, clearingPublic ledger in real time (block explorer)

Card Rails vs Stablecoin Ramps

Dimension Card rails Stablecoin ramps (what I saw)
Getting money inCard purchase: auth + captureA card-funded on-ramp is a card purchase, with the provider as merchant. Same auth, 3DS, issuer declines
Getting money outRefund to the original card. Merchant funds settled by the acquirer to a bank accountPayout is a push to card (Visa Direct, itself card rails) or a bank transfer
Risk signalsBIN country, IP, 3DSSame signals. The transaction's country followed the customer's KYC country, not the IP (a UK VPN didn't change it)
Cross-borderFX surcharge, scheme rulesA EUR payout to a US card was refused as cross-border
FeesMostly a % of the amount (MDR), sometimes plus a fixed fee, paid by the merchantPaid by the customer: a % with a minimum (sell 1%, card buy ~3–3.6%, €3.99 minimum), plus network fees independent of the amount
SpeedAuth instant, settlement T+1/T+2Transfer in seconds, then minutes for the confirmations the provider requires (12 for ETH). Fiat payout: minutes (card) to days (bank transfer)
Compliance and liabilitySplit: the issuer KYCs the cardholder, the acquirer KYBs the merchant, the merchant carries chargebacks (shifted to the issuer with 3DS)Concentrated in the ramp provider: customer KYC, AML, licences, fraud and chargebacks. MoonPay acts as merchant of record; the integrating app only goes through the provider's KYB
Failure modeDecline, reversal, chargebackCharged but not delivered. Deposited but not paid out. Refund approved but not sent
